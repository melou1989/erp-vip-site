ERP Implementation Partner: How to Choose (and What to Avoid)

PUBLISHED August 13, 2026 · GUIDE / Partner Selection

ERP software mostly runs the same way once it's installed. The difference between a project that's late and one that isn't is usually the implementation partner, not the version or the cloud deployment. That's why the selection process should be aimed at the people who will do the work, not at the slideware sales team.

First, break down the proposal by named individuals. You want to know the project manager's track record, the lead consultant's industry experience, and whether any of the work is being subcontracted. Good partners will tell you who isn't available. Bad ones have a roster of so-called certified experts who only show up for the kickoff. Make the named team a contractual thing, not a wish.

Then force the partner to explain how they actually handle the messy parts. Data migration, legacy integrations, change requests, and user acceptance testing are where ERP projects go to die. A partner that can walk you through their exact testing sign-off process and tell you what their role is when something breaks is worth far more than one that promises "best practices" and a bright future. Ask for examples from their last project in your industry, including what went wrong.

You also need to distinguish between a fixed-bid quote and a time-and-materials one. Fixed bids can freeze scope in ways that make every legitimate change feel like an argument. T&M can drift. Neither is inherently better; what matters is whether the contract includes a workable change order process and a realistic timetable. Check references. Did they lose consultants mid-project? Did the partner keep their commitments after the go-live? A good partner tells you what you'll have to give up before you sign. The rest are just selling implementation hours.
