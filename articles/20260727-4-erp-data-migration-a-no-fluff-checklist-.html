ERP Data Migration: A No-Fluff Checklist to Sidestep Failure

PUBLISHED July 27, 2026 · GUIDE / Checklist

Most ERP projects don't fail because of the software. They fail because the data arrives broken, incomplete, or in the wrong shape. Vendors love to talk about their shiny new modules, but the dirty secret is that migration is where implementations go to die. I've watched teams spend six months on configuration and two weeks on data — then wonder why the go-live turns into a fire drill. The checklist below skips the fluff and targets the failure points you'll actually face.

First, get your source data into a staging environment before you touch the target system. This sounds obvious, but I've seen teams point migration tools directly at production databases, then scramble when a field mapping breaks everything. Use a staging area to profile, clean, and transform data without risk. Pay particular attention to customer and vendor master records — duplicates and missing tax IDs are the most common tripwires. Run a simple distinct count on primary keys. If you see 10% more rows than expected, you've already found a problem.

Mapping is where most of the real work lives, and it's rarely a one-pass exercise. The classic mistake is to map fields in a spreadsheet, hand it off to a developer, and declare victory. Instead, iterate: map a subset, run a test load, inspect the output, adjust. Repeat. Involve the people who actually use the data — a purchasing manager will catch a vendor classification error that an IT generalist never sees. Also, plan for historical data retention. Many teams purge old transactions to simplify migration, only to discover during an audit that compliance requires seven years of purchase orders. Decide your retention policy before you touch the first extract.

Cutover planning is the moment where every shortcut comes due. The worst failure point? Underestimating the time needed to validate the final load. Budget half a day for every 50,000 records you migrate, minimum — and that assumes your mapping is solid. Build a rollback plan before you press the button. If you can't revert within four hours, you're not ready. Finally, test the migration with a full dress rehearsal at least twice, using production-size data. The first run will reveal holes in your validation scripts. The second run should be a formality. If it isn't, postpone the go-live. Nothing is more expensive than a botched cutover.
