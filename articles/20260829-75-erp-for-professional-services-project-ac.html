ERP for Professional Services: Project Accounting Needs

PUBLISHED August 29, 2026 · INSIGHTS / Vendor Selection

Professional services firms sell a peculiar kind of inventory: hours, expertise, judgment. Once the work is done, it's gone. So the accounting system has to track the money as it moves through a project lifecycle — from proposal and contract to time capture, billing, and final revenue recognition. If the ERP treats project costs like ordinary COGS, it will misstate profitability from day one.

Generic ERPs handle revenue and expenses fine, but project accounting changes the rules. You need to tie timesheets and expenses to specific contracts, recognize revenue under ASC 606 or IFRS 15, and handle billing on milestone, fixed-fee, or time-and-materials arrangements. Deltek and Unanet built their products around these workflows. Microsoft Dynamics 365 and NetSuite have project accounting modules, but you'll likely need significant configuration to match your firm's billing and recognition rules. That's not a red flag on those vendors; it's just the reality of how specialized professional services accounting has become.

A bigger decision is whether the ERP includes resource scheduling and skills matching. That's often why IT directors and CFOs fail to get buy-in: they buy a project accounting system, but the consultants and project managers want to see availability, forecast utilization, and planned versus actual hours. Some ERPs handle this natively; others require an integrated third-party PSA tool. Decide before you shortlist whether resource management is a finance problem, an operations problem, or both.

Pricing varies significantly by vendor and deployment size. The bigger trap is implementation scope. Most project accounting implementations take longer than estimated because the finance team and the delivery team don't speak the same language: CFOs care about revenue recognition and WIP; project managers care about budget remaining and resource conflicts. If you don't define that up front, the ERP project will stall while each side argues over what 'on track' means.
