How to Choose an ERP Implementation Partner (Not Just a Reseller)

PUBLISHED July 28, 2026 · GUIDE / Strategy Guide

The biggest mistake buyers make is treating an implementation partner like a box to check. You’re not hiring a company; you’re hiring the people who will sit in your conference rooms for the next year. Vendor logos on a website tell you nothing about whether that partner actually knows how to handle a messy data migration or a finance team that refuses to change its chart of accounts. Start by demanding the resumes of the actual project team — not the sales team. If the partner hesitates or gives you generic profiles, that’s your first red flag.

Methodology matters, but not in the way most RFPs assume. Every partner claims to follow some named framework — Agile, Waterfall, hybrid. What you actually want to see is their last three post-implementation reviews. Ask for them. If they can’t or won’t share, it means either they don’t track outcomes or their outcomes are bad. A good partner will show you where they went wrong, not just their success stories. Also: look at how they handle scope creep in writing, not just in sales pitches. The partner who hands you a change-order form on day one of a workshop is the one who knows what’s coming.

Pricing varies significantly by vendor and deployment size, but there’s a more telling signal: the ratio of senior to junior consultants on your account. Many large firms staff projects with recent graduates and a single part-time architect. That works fine for a standard rollout. For a company with custom workflows, legacy integrations, or complex regulatory requirements, you need gray hair in the room. Ask specifically how many hours your project will see from someone with more than 10 years of ERP implementation experience. If that number is below 20%, negotiate or walk.

Trust your gut about cultural fit. A partner who talks over your team during the demo will talk over them during cutover. A partner who pushes back hard on your timeline before signing is either honest or rigid — both can be fine, but you need to know which. The real test happens during a mock go-live or a crisis simulation. Some partners will run one of these as part of their proposal process. Others will offer it only if you ask. Ask.
