Post Go-Live ERP Support: What to Plan For

PUBLISHED October 06, 2026 · GUIDE / Support Planning

Go-live is a milestone, not an ending. The cutover weekend gets all the attention, but the weeks after it decide whether finance can close, warehouse can ship, and payroll can run. Hypercare is the short period when the implementation team stays close, usually with daily triage and faster response. The trap is treating hypercare as the support model. It is a bridge. Before cutover, you need a named owner for post go-live support, a ticket path for every module, and a decision process for changes that arrive as 'small fixes.'

Staffing is where most plans get thin. A support desk that only knows how to reset passwords will not help when the AP match exception report returns bad data or an integration drops sales orders. Build a first line of super users who know the business process, a second line of internal analysts who can inspect configuration and data, and a third line that reaches the ERP vendor or implementation partner. Super users need backfill. If the same person is expected to close the books and answer every ticket, both jobs slip. Train at least two people per critical process, not one.

Define severity, not just urgency. A report showing the wrong subtotal at month-end may be a nuisance; a blocked invoice posting or failed payment file is not. Put severity definitions in writing with examples from your business. Response time and resolution time are different commitments. Vendors and partners often separate how-to questions, break/fix defects, and enhancements, and each may have different channels or fees. Read the support contract before go-live: named contacts, hours, escalation paths, and what happens when the partner's original team rolls off. Customizations, integrations, and reports are usually your responsibility unless the contract says otherwise.

Knowledge transfer is not a folder of PDFs. It is the ability to answer why a tax code, approval rule, or inventory movement behaves the way it does. Capture configuration decisions, integration maps, data flows, security roles, batch jobs, and report logic while the implementers are still available. Record short screen captures for recurring tasks: month-end close, payment runs, cycle counts, interface error handling. Build runbooks that a new hire can follow. Then assign owners. Documentation with no owner becomes stale in one release cycle.

Post go-live support costs money, and the bill does not stop at the vendor's maintenance fee. Vendor support and subscription pricing vary significantly by vendor and deployment size. Managed services contracts might be a monthly retainer, ticket blocks, or dedicated staff. Third-party support can cover some legacy systems or provide lower-cost break/fix, but it may not deliver patches, tax updates, or access to new releases. The practical question is not 'cheapest support?' It is which issues must go to the vendor, which can go to a partner, and which your team should own. Write that split down.

Governance keeps support from becoming an endless queue. Track tickets by module, root cause, reopen rate, and age. Review the list monthly with finance, operations, and IT. If the same training issue appears again and again, fix the training or the screen, not the ticket. If every fix is urgent, your prioritization is broken. Plan for patching and upgrades too. Cloud ERP releases arrive on a cadence you partly control, and on-prem systems still need security and tax updates. Every patch deserves a regression test plan, even a short one. The first month-end close, first audit request, and first upgrade will expose gaps in the support plan. Better to find them on paper before cutover.

Write the support plan before cutover. Negotiate the transition while the implementation partner still wants a reference and your super users still remember the design sessions. After go-live, everyone is tired, the queue is live, and the only thing that matters is who picks up the ticket.
