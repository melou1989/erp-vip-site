Implementation Teams: What Enterprises Get Wrong in RFPs

PUBLISHED September 24, 2026 · NEWS / Implementation

Enterprise software buyers issue RFPs as a matter of course. Implementation teams, though, see a familiar split: plenty of documents get circulated, few help the organization make a better decision. The headline and summary point to that gap without pretending it has a single cause.

From the implementation side, the frustration usually isn't that an RFP exists. It's that the document can become a feature checklist disconnected from process ownership, data readiness, and who will actually run the system after go-live. Vendors answer what's asked. They can't fix what the buyer never defined.

The summary's line is blunt: RFPs are common; truly successful ones less so. That should be a warning to any organization treating the RFP as a procurement formality rather than a working plan for implementation.
