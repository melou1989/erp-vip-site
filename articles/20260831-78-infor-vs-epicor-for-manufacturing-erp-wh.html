Infor vs Epicor for Manufacturing ERP: Which Fits Your Plant?

PUBLISHED August 31, 2026 · COMPARISON / Vendor Comparison

It is tempting to group Infor and Epicor together because both sell manufacturing ERP and rarely appear on the same deal as SAP or Oracle. Beyond that, the two vendors have very different instincts. Infor builds industry-specific applications and pushes multi-tenant cloud hard. Epicor starts from the plant floor and grows upward, which explains why its system feels closer to how a shop actually runs.

For make-to-order or engineer-to-order work, Infor CloudSuite Industrial—the old SyteLine, still alive in that package—has a real edge. Its job costing, shop-floor scheduling, and product configurator are built for jobs that move in nonstandard ways and eat up materials in batches that don't match a BOM. Epicor handles this kind of work too, but it is more comfortable with mixed-mode production: a plant that runs some repetitive lines, some discrete work, some make-to-stock, all under one roof. If your manufacturing reality looks like a zoo, Epicor will ask fewer questions.

The cloud story is where the vendors diverge strategically. Infor runs on AWS and sells multi-tenant SaaS, with a common tech platform called Infor OS. It is a full commitment; upgrades are continuous and you should expect data-model choices dictated by the vertical solution. Epicor Kinetic runs on a newer architecture that feels more like a software product than a vertical bundle. In practice, implementers often get Epicor live in a shorter timeline, but that speed comes with trade-offs—the configurable core can be customized into a brittle shape if a partner treats it as a blank slate.

The honest question for an operations leader is not which vendor has more features, because both have plenty. It is whether you believe your manufacturing process is a variation of an industry standard, or a genuinely special operation. The first group tends to thrive on Infor; the second tends to fight it. Epicor is usually the smarter match when you want a flexible nucleus and a seat at the table over how the system gets tailored. Your consultants and IT directors should be evaluating their own comfort with tight vertical alignment versus open-ended configurability—that decision will drive the project before a single module is turned on.
