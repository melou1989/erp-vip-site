Why Project Accounting Needs Its Own ERP Brain for Professional Services

PUBLISHED July 28, 2026 · INSIGHTS / Industry Insights

Most ERP systems were born in manufacturing or distribution. They know how to track a widget from raw material to finished good. But a professional services firm doesn't ship widgets — it sells hours, expertise, and fixed-price outcomes. The accounting motions are fundamentally different. Yet many firms still try to force-fit a general ledger designed for inventory into a world of time sheets, utilization rates, and percentage-of-completion revenue recognition. The result: manual workarounds, billing errors, and a finance team that spends more time reconciling than analyzing.

Project accounting isn't just a module — it's a different set of bones. The core necessity is a system that can capture time and expenses against a project, track budget vs. actuals in real time, and handle multiple billing methods (time-and-materials, fixed price, milestone, retainer) within the same engagement. Revenue recognition under ASC 606 and IFRS 15 adds another layer: you need to estimate total project costs, recognize revenue based on progress, and adjust when estimates change. Deltek has built its entire business around this — its Vantagepoint and Ajera products are purpose-built for architecture, engineering, and consulting firms. Oracle NetSuite offers a SuiteSuccess for Services Industries, but it takes careful configuration to avoid the gaps in its standard project management tools. Microsoft Dynamics 365 Project Operations sits somewhere in between, strong on integration with Office 365 and Power BI, but the learning curve for resource scheduling and cost allocation can be steep.

The real friction point for most firms isn't the software's feature list — it's the disconnect between the project manager's view and the accountant's view. PMs want to know if a project is running over hours today. The finance team needs to know if this month's recognizeable revenue matches the work actually performed. If those two datasets come from different systems, or even from different reports in the same ERP, arguments break out at month-end close. The best deployments force a single source of truth: time entered by a consultant flows directly into project cost, which feeds the revenue recognition engine, which triggers the invoice. Any break in that chain means someone is manually copying numbers from a spreadsheet into a journal entry.

Vendors have started responding. SAP now sells a Professional Services industry add-on for S/4HANA that includes resource-related billing and project-based revenue accounting. But add-ons mean extra licensing and integration work — and they often lag behind the core product updates. Forbess, a mid-market advisory firm, famously spent two years customizing Microsoft Dynamics AX before finally migrating to a dedicated PSA platform. The pattern repeats: firms try to stretch their existing ERP, hit a ceiling, then rip and replace. If you're evaluating software, look first at how the system handles three things: automatically recognizing revenue on partial deliveries of services, re-budgeting when a project scope changes mid-stream, and invoicing via multiple methods on the same project. If those workflows require a custom script or a third-party connector, keep looking.
