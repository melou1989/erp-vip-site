Katana vs NetSuite for Small Manufacturers: Which Fits?

PUBLISHED August 09, 2026 · COMPARISON / Comparison

Katana is a scheduling-first inventory and MRP tool aimed at small make-to-order and mixed-mode manufacturers. Its live shop floor board, drag-and-drop scheduling, and stock visibility make it easy to answer 'what can we ship next' without an implementation team. NetSuite is a full ERP with financial consolidation, fixed assets, multi-subsidiary accounting, and a manufacturing-heavy module set. The distinction matters because a small manufacturer often buys software to solve one pain first.

The practical gap shows up in daily use. Katana's scheduling board is built for production planners who need to reschedule jobs when a machine breaks or material arrives late. It handles material availability and purchase orders but does not try to be the system of record for CFO-level close. NetSuite's work order and WIP functionality gets a lot more accounting detail: you can track labor and overhead variances, route steps, and see landed costs accumulate. For a job shop that lives and dies on 'when is it done,' Katana feels immediate. For a business whose bank or investors demand monthly financials with inventory valuation, NetSuite is the more usual answer.

Most small manufacturers don't need all of NetSuite's finance machinery. But they need some. Katana integrates with accounting packages like Xero and QuickBooks, which handles that layer for many, but the integration is not the same as native financial logic. NetSuite's real edge is avoiding a two-system mess when you outgrow QuickBooks. The downside: NetSuite's implementation complexity and consulting load are real, even with SuiteSuccess packaging. Katana's setup is typically measured in days; NetSuite's often in months, depending on the modules you choose.

If you're evaluating, start with the pain. If work orders are still on a whiteboard and inventory numbers live in a spreadsheet, Katana will fix that faster and with less pain than any full ERP. If you already struggle with multi-subsidiary accounting, revenue recognition, or complex inventory valuation, Katana won't be enough and NetSuite is the direction to look. In between is the tricky place: a small manufacturer that wants to grow into a bigger one. There you want to know whether your future CFO is going to sign off on the books from a shop-floor system or demand a finance-first ERP.
