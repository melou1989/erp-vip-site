ERP for Construction: Job Costing and Change Orders

PUBLISHED October 04, 2026 · INSIGHTS / Evaluation

Construction job costing is not general ledger with a project tag bolted on. A real job cost ledger tracks cost codes, phases, categories, commitments, actuals, and cost-to-complete at a granularity that lets a project manager see margin before the month closes. Committed costs come from purchase orders and subcontracts. Actuals come from AP invoices, payroll, equipment charges, and sometimes inventory. If the ERP only shows actuals, you are steering with a rearview mirror. Approved change orders need to hit the budget and the contract value at the same time, or the job cost report and the billing report start telling different stories.

Change orders are where most construction ERPs show their age. A change order is rarely one clean document. It starts as an RFI, a field directive, a change order request, a potential change order, or a subcontractor change order. Some get approved quickly; some sit pending for months. The ERP needs to track those pending changes without pretending they are revenue, then convert them into approved owner change orders or subcontract change orders that update commitments, budgets, and billing. When project management tracks changes in one system and accounting re-enters them in another, markups get dropped, subcontractor back-charges get missed, and the WIP schedule becomes a monthly argument instead of a report.

Evaluate the cost code model before you look at dashboards. Can you map cost codes to GL accounts without duplicating them? Can you track labor burden, union benefits, prevailing wage, equipment rates, and overhead allocation? Can a superintendent enter time daily against the right cost code and phase, or does payroll wait for paper timecards? Construction ERPs such as Viewpoint Vista, Sage 300 CRE, CMiC, Acumatica Construction, Deltek ComputerEase, Foundation, and Penta have different strengths, but the core question is the same: does committed cost, actual cost, and cost-to-complete live in one place? If not, the PM builds a spreadsheet and the ERP becomes a tax compliance tool.

Change order approval is a workflow problem as much as an accounting problem. Who can approve? At what dollar threshold? Does a pending change order reserve contingency? Does an approved change order create a new billing line, revise the subcontract, and update the job budget automatically? Ask to see the audit trail. Can you tell who changed the amount, when, and why? Can you report pending change orders by age, project, owner, and subcontractor? Contractors lose money on change orders not because they forget to bill them, but because the left hand approved the work while the right hand never updated the commitment. The ERP should make that gap visible on a daily basis, not after the project manager calls a meeting.

Integration matters, but do not confuse integration with a data model. Procore, Autodesk Construction Cloud, and Trimble ProjectSight can run field workflows and push change orders to an ERP. Some construction ERPs also have their own project management modules. Either approach can work. The failure mode is an integration that syncs only approved change orders and ignores pending changes. Another failure mode is syncing cost codes as free-text fields. The result is a job cost report that looks complete but is missing the very changes that will decide whether the project makes money. Ask how the systems handle deletions, revisions, and rejected change orders. That is where syncs break.

When you demo, bring a real project, not a vendor's sample data. Include a mix of subcontracts, owner change orders, pending changes, retainage, and a cost code structure that reflects how your field teams actually work. Ask the vendor to enter a timecard, post an AP invoice, create a subcontract change order, create an owner change order, run a job cost report, and produce a WIP schedule. Watch how many screens it takes and whether the change order automatically updates the commitment. If the vendor needs a solution engineer to make the flow look easy, your accounting team will need a consultant every month. Pricing varies significantly by vendor, user count, and deployment model. Implementation effort depends on data migration, cost code redesign, and how many systems you are replacing. The software will not fix a broken change order process. It will expose it. That is useful, but only if you are ready to change the process, not just the screens.
