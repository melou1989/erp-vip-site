Signs It's Time to Replace Your Legacy ERP System

PUBLISHED August 25, 2026 · GUIDE / Checklist

The first sign is usually the patchwork. If you maintain a middleware layer that shuttles data between your ERP and the modern SaaS tools everyone else uses, and that middleware demands more attention than the ERP itself, you already know replacement is coming. Someone is writing scripts that break every time a vendor updates an API.

The second sign is buried in compliance. Legacy systems accumulate custom tables and code branches that only two people still understand—and neither of them works in your office. When finance closes the books but then spends three weeks producing an audit file, because the company's actual transactions are scattered across five interfaces and a dozen Excel uploads, that is not an operational quirk. That is a regulatory exposure.

Vendors are also making the decision for you. SAP has publicly committed to ending mainstream maintenance for ECC 6.0 in 2027. Microsoft has been steering Dynamics GP customers toward Dynamics 365 Business Central. Oracle has set long-term support horizons for older EBS releases. Those dates may slip, but the direction does not. Every year you stay put, the cost of an eventual migration changes shape: an already ageing implementation gets harder to staff, and the security review for any new customer or bank starts with an awkward conversation about unsupported software.

Then there is the quiet one: your own teams. If your ERP consultants spend more time reading old source code than proposing functional changes, if the average age of your customizations is older than the employees who submit change tickets, and if one year of maintenance plus support extensions now comes close to the projected cost of a new deployment—pricing varies wildly by vendor and scale, but the comparison is worth doing—then replacement is less of a risk than staying still.
