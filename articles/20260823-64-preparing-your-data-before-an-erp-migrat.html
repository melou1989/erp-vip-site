Preparing Your Data Before an ERP Migration: A Practical Guide

PUBLISHED August 23, 2026 · GUIDE / Data Strategy

Most ERP projects slip because of data, not software. The payroll file with employee IDs in five formats. The customer master where Acme and Acme Inc. are separate accounts. By the time you're reconciling balances in a parallel test, the cleanup work you skipped upstream becomes a daily tax on your finance team. Good data preparation doesn't just make migration smoother—it determines whether the new system can do what you bought it for.

Start with an audit of what you actually have. Inventory every source system: your current ERP, that aging Access database in operations, spreadsheets attached to emails. For each data entity—customers, vendors, items, chart of accounts, open transactions—get counts and identify who owns the data definition. Then look for the things that will break in the new system: missing tax IDs, inactive records, duplicate classifications, inconsistent units of measure. Do not let a project manager wave this off as an IT task. The business decides which duplicates are real and which are legitimate.

Before any cleansing, decide what the target data model actually requires. ERP vendors document required fields, but your business has its own mandatory logic—some customers must have a billing address, every item needs a commodity code, every open invoice needs a due date. Map each legacy field to a target field, and be explicit about what gets dropped. A surprising volume of old data is not worth carrying over; archived history can live in a read-only system. Carrying everything into the new ERP out of fear is how you end up with a database as messy as the old one, just more expensive.

Test the data as a batch, not as a side note. Run your cleaned files through a mock migration into sandbox, then verify counts, referential integrity, and totals against source. Let key users challenge the results with their own reports. This is also where you'll discover the data you didn't know was missing—the customer master references a sales rep code that doesn't exist, or open purchase orders point at a closed item. Every issue you find in a test run is one less issue you'll explain to the CFO during month-end close. Data preparation is unglamorous, but it's the part of an ERP project where the real money is spent or saved.
