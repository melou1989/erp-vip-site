Dynamics GP vs Business Central: Should You Migrate?

PUBLISHED September 23, 2026 · COMPARISON / Comparison

Dynamics GP and Dynamics 365 Business Central get compared constantly, but they were never really rivals. GP is a Great Plains product from the mid-1990s that Microsoft bought in 2001. Business Central is the descendant of Navision, rebuilt as a cloud service and rebranded in 2018. They occupy overlapping midmarket territory because Microsoft owns both, not because a buyer ever chose between them on the merits. The comparison matters now for one reason: GP has a horizon and Business Central does not.

Microsoft has been clear that GP ends at version 18.x. There will be no 19. Mainstream support has already wound down for the current release, and extended support carries into the late 2020s, which means security fixes and tax and regulatory updates continue but feature development does not. This is not abandonment, and describing it as such is lazy. It is something stranger: a stable product that will never get better. If a GP process annoys your finance team today, no future release will fix it.

The real pressure comes from the add-on ecosystem, not the support calendar. GP's strength was always its ISVs, a dense field of partners covering payroll, sales tax, document capture, collections, field service, distribution and warehouse edge cases. Those companies run their own math on how many GP customers remain and when development stops paying for itself. When one sunsets a GP product, you typically learn about it at renewal, not at a keynote. A handful of those announcements will do more to trigger a migration decision than any Microsoft roadmap slide.

Business Central is a different animal in almost every operational respect. It is sold as a subscription, runs on Azure, and receives two major releases a year that arrive whether or not you feel ready, with a testing window before each. Customization happens through extensions rather than edits to base code, which is the single biggest cultural change for a GP shop that has been modifying objects since the Bush administration. Licensing splits into Essentials and Premium tiers, with manufacturing and service management sitting in the higher one. An on-premises deployment still exists, but it is a different licensing structure and, in practice, a different staffing commitment.

Functionally, the overlap is larger than GP veterans expect and the gaps are narrower but sharper. Financials, purchasing, sales, inventory, basic production and directed warehouse flows are all there. Where teams get surprised: there is no native US payroll in Business Central, so you are buying an ISV or handing payroll to a service. Deep intercompany structures that a GP shop assembled over fifteen years with a mix of native features and customizations rarely map cleanly onto dimensions and consolidation in Business Central. Analytical Accounting has no direct twin. Management Reporter gives way to Financial Reporting and Power BI, which sounds like an upgrade until you count the reports someone has to rebuild by hand.

Migration is the wrong word for what actually happens, and vendors who use it are being loose with your budget. There is no supported tool that converts a GP company into a Business Central company. You reimplement: redesign the chart of accounts, reload master data, bring over open transactions and balances, and usually keep GP running read-only for historical lookup. Most companies run parallel for a period. And your GP add-ons do not travel, so every one of them gets re-evaluated, replaced, or rebuilt. Cost and duration vary enormously with entity count, add-on sprawl and how much custom code is embedded in your current system, so treat any vendor who quotes a fixed timeline before scoping as a risk.

In practice, the decision usually comes down to two questions rather than five. Can you still hire people who know GP, and are your critical add-ons still being sold? If both answers are yes, staying on 18.x through extended support is a defensible choice, particularly for a stable single-entity business with modest reporting needs. If either answer is no, the support deadline is a distraction from a problem you already have. Worth adding: Business Central is not the only landing spot. NetSuite, Acumatica, Sage Intacct and Dynamics 365 Finance all show up in these evaluations for good reasons, and picking Business Central mainly because the logo is already familiar is how companies end up doing this twice.

One exercise earns its keep before any demos. List every third-party add-on in your GP environment, then ask each vendor in writing when they intend to stop supporting GP and what their Business Central story is. That list is your actual migration timeline.
