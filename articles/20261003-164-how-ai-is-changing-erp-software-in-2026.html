How AI Is Changing ERP Software in 2026

PUBLISHED October 03, 2026 · INSIGHTS / Strategy

The AI story in ERP has moved past the chat-with-your-data demo. In 2026, SAP Joule, Oracle Fusion AI agents, Microsoft Dynamics 365 Copilot, Workday Illuminate, Infor Coleman and GenAI, and NetSuite AI features are aimed at specific steps: matching invoices, drafting journal entries, flagging supply-chain exceptions, recommending maintenance windows, and explaining why margin moved with context from the ledger. That shift matters. A copilot that summarizes a variance is nice. An agent that creates a payment proposal, routes it for approval, and logs the audit trail changes who does the work.

The real change is not the model. It is where AI sits in the process. ERP is still the system of record, but vendors want it to become a system of action. Agents need permissions, workflow state, and clean master data to do anything useful. If your customer records have three spellings, your supplier list is stale, or your chart of accounts grew wild, AI will automate the mess faster. That is why process mining and data governance budgets are quietly rising alongside AI line items.

Finance is the first battlefield. AP automation already used OCR and rules; generative AI adds tolerance for messy invoices, email threads, and contract terms. In AR and collections, agents can prioritize accounts, draft reminders, and suggest payment plans. In close, AI can propose reconciliations and explain fluctuations. The catch: auditors want traceability. The model said so is not a control. IT directors should ask whether AI actions are logged, whether humans approve high-risk entries, and whether the vendor trains models on tenant data. Microsoft, SAP, Oracle, and Workday have published different positions on data use; read the contract, not the keynote.

Supply chain and operations get less attention but may see more value. Demand planning has used statistical models for years. What is new is combining those forecasts with supplier emails, weather feeds, and production delays, then letting an agent propose reroutes or purchase order changes. In manufacturing, AI-assisted maintenance can turn sensor noise into work orders. In distribution, agents can quote available-to-promise dates across warehouses. These use cases fail when the ERP data is fragmented across plants or when approvals still live in email.

Buying advice. Most ERP vendors now bundle some AI features into premium tiers or consumption-based pricing. Pricing varies significantly by vendor, deployment size, and how much inference runs inside the ERP. Ask for a written list: which AI features are included, which cost extra, what happens if usage spikes, and how AI affects renewal negotiations. Also test the ugly cases: What happens when an agent proposes a duplicate payment? Can you pause a workflow? Can you export the reasoning? Who owns the output? Those questions matter more than a polished demo.

The practical outcome for 2026 is uneven. Some teams will run AI agents in AP, collections, and planning with real ROI. Others will spend a year cleaning supplier records and security roles before an agent can be trusted. The vendors that win will not be the ones with the loudest AI branding. They will be the ones that make AI work inside the ERP's existing controls, audit trails, and data model. That is less exciting than a keynote. It is also where the work gets done.
