ERP Requirements Gathering: A Step-by-Step Approach

PUBLISHED September 01, 2026 · GUIDE / Strategy

The fastest route to a failed ERP selection is downloading a generic requirements template from the web and filling it out over a weekend. The document that comes out of that exercise reflects nothing about how your operation actually works. It's a shopping list, not a specification. Before you talk to a single vendor, you need to ground the process in your own processes.

Start with interviews. Not with the CFO and the IT director—with the people who touch the system every day. The warehouse lead who has to check inventory against three different spreadsheets. The AR clerk who manually reconciles payments. They'll tell you what actually breaks. Their list won't be glamorous, but it's the one that matters if you're trying to avoid implementation rework.

After the interviews, group what you heard into two buckets: mandatory and negotiable. Mandatory means the business genuinely cannot run without it—not 'we'd really like it.' Negotiable covers the rest. Then map each mandatory item to a business process. If a requirement doesn't tie to a process step, it probably belongs in the trash. This is also the point where you walk the warehouse floor and watch one shift before you finalize anything.

When your draft is done, have a handful of people from the operations side annotate it—not executives, the people who'll live with the system. Then cut it. Every requirement you keep costs you time in demos, scoring, and negotiations. The goal is a document that's tight enough that two vendors can respond to it without calling you for clarification on everything. That's when you're ready to send it to the market.
