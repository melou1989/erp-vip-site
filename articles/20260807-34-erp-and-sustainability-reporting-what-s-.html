ERP and Sustainability Reporting: What's Changing

PUBLISHED August 07, 2026 · INSIGHTS / Strategy

For most of the last decade, sustainability reporting in an ERP context meant exporting a few tables to Excel and sending them to a consultant. That era is ending. Not because software vendors suddenly care about the planet, but because the regulations now demand it. The EU's CSRD, the ISSB's IFRS S1 and S2, and California's climate bills all require data that is comparable, consistent, and — crucially — auditable. Double materiality means you have to report on both how climate affects your business and how your business affects the climate. Spreadsheets can't hold that line.

So ERP vendors are integrating sustainability into the ledger. SAP has its Sustainability Control Tower and, more recently, embedded capabilities in S/4HANA Cloud. Oracle has Sustainability Management in Fusion Cloud. Microsoft has Cloud for Sustainability, which sits on Dataverse and draws from Dynamics 365 and other sources. Workday is adding emissions tracking for workforce and facilities. Whether these are genuinely useful or just label-printing depends on implementation, but the direction is clear: sustainability data is being treated like financial data, not a separate reporting project.

The hard part is scope 3. Your ERP can tell you exactly how much electricity your factories used, but it can't tell you what your suppliers emitted unless they give you that data. That's why the bigger change is happening in data standards and supply chain collaboration, not just in the ERP module. Vendors are building supplier portals and data exchange networks. But the quality of output still depends on what you put in. If your foundational master data — vendors, materials, routing — is messy, then your emissions calculations will be messy. No AI layer fixes that.

What this means for IT directors and CFOs: don't wait for the perfect all-in-one sustainability suite. Start with the data you already control, get your unit-level energy and material flows mapped, and then decide what you need to purchase. Pricing for these modules varies significantly by vendor and deployment size, so treat each proposal on its own claims. And remember that assurance is coming. If your sustainability report will be audited the way your financial statements are, you need an audit trail. That's the real change — not nicer dashboards.
