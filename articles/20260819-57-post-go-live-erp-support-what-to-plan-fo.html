Post Go-Live ERP Support: What to Plan For

PUBLISHED August 19, 2026 · GUIDE / Support Strategy

Go-live is when the real system starts running, but it's also when the project team starts disappearing. Hypercare is the term most vendors and consultancies use for the first weeks after cutover, and if you haven't defined it as a triage period with clear severity levels and named owners, you'll spend that time arguing about who fixes the broken purchase order interface. The people who built the system are still around, but their attention is already splitting across the next customer or the next internal project. Make hypercare specific: which issues get routed to consultants, which go to your internal team, and how long each response actually takes.

Once hypercare ends, the support model changes shape. The vendor has a service desk and an SLA for standard tickets, but those SLAs are measured from ticket acceptance, not from user frustration. Internal support becomes the buffer between users and the vendor, so somebody needs to own that buffer full-time. The common mistake is treating the ERP support role as a part-time assignment for someone who also does financial reporting. That person will become a bottleneck, and the vendors will be happy to sell you extra hours while your users wait. If you have custom integrations or in-house modifications, plan for them separately. Standard modules get patches and version updates; custom code is your problem before the vendor's.

A few months after go-live, the data issues start to surface. Some are legacy data that was cleaned poorly during migration. Others are new problems created by users who found workarounds the project team never tested. You need a data governance process that isn't just a monthly deletion script. Someone has to decide which records get corrected, who is allowed to change master data, and what happens when a user enters a vendor invoice without the correct tax code. That doesn't sound complicated until it's quarter-end and the ERP won't close because a handful of transactions are stuck in an approval workflow.

Meanwhile, the system itself needs regular maintenance: vendor releases, tax rate updates, year-end closing routines, regulatory changes. If those tasks aren't on a calendar with assigned owners, they arrive as emergencies. No one sends a reminder that the new VAT rules start next month. The first two quarters after go-live will show you what your support model actually costs, not in the contract, but in overtime, audit adjustments, and the quiet workaround culture that forms when users don't trust the system. If the support plan only covers helpdesk call volume, the first quarter-end will teach you what you missed.
