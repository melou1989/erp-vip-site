ERP for Startups: When Is It Too Early?

PUBLISHED August 05, 2026 · INSIGHTS / Strategy

Ask a handful of founders when they think about ERP, and most will say 'later.' The real answer is messier. ERP is not a stage a startup reaches; it's a tool you pull in when the cost of spreadsheets and disconnected systems starts eating your margins. The problem is that by the time that cost is obvious, you're already losing time you can't buy back.

Too early looks like this: one legal entity, one currency, no inventory — or inventory you can count in a day. Your finance close takes a couple of days. QuickBooks or Xero works, and your accountant doesn't hate it. If that's you, an ERP will slow you down. Those systems assume stable processes. Startups change processes weekly. You don't want a system that makes you file a change request in Jira before you can change a lead status.

The shift happens when the pain changes shape. You've got a second entity because a foreign customer needed an invoice. You have intercompany loans and transfers. Inventory is in three warehouses and a co-packer's facility. Your controller is re-entering data from Shopify, Stripe, and a custom CRM into QuickBooks, and the CFO still can't answer 'what's our gross margin by product line?' That's the moment spreadsheets stop being a convenience and start being a risk.

So don't ask 'when is the right time.' Ask 'what is the cost of the current mess?' If that cost is a few hours a week, wait. If it's a late night close, errored checks, or an auditor's finding, it's time. When you do it, don't rush to the most complicated system. Look at NetSuite, Microsoft Business Central, or a vertical solution that fits your industry. Plan on migration taking twice as long as you think. And rope in someone who has actually implemented the software before — most startup founders haven't.
