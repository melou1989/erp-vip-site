Post-Go-Live ERP Support: What to Plan For

PUBLISHED August 03, 2026 · GUIDE / Strategy

Go-live is a deployment, not a completion. The cutover closes one project and opens another: support and stabilization. In practice, the first weeks after go-live show what actually breaks — integration jobs that worked in test die under real data volumes, users find workarounds that bypass controls, and the vendor patch schedule doesn't pause for your internal training plan.

Plan support around three layers: the vendor's maintenance contract, the implementation partner's hypercare, and your internal IT team. Each layer has a different response. Vendor support handles code-level defects in standard software; the partner handles defects in your configuration and customizations; internal IT handles access, data, and everything the other two don't recognize. The confusion between these layers causes more escalation delays than actual software bugs.

The worst support gap is custom code. Standard ERP patches and quarterly updates are manageable; custom extensions are not. They break in unpredictable ways and the vendor won't take responsibility. Before go-live, write down who owns each customization, how it's tested against new releases, and what happens if the original developer leaves. If your implementation partner is billing by the hour for post-go-live work, get a fixed-price hypercare agreement for a defined window.

Also plan for user behavior. The project team fixes what users report, but many issues never surface as tickets — users quietly rekey data in Excel. Build a post-go-live feedback loop: floor walks, support tickets that feed a fix list, and a monthly review of the most common workarounds. The goal is not to achieve a tidy "steady state"; it's to make the handoff from project to operations explicit, funded, and time-boxed. Once that's done, the next upgrade decision becomes just another project.
