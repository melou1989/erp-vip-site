Sage Intacct vs NetSuite: Which Financial Management ERP Wins?

PUBLISHED August 03, 2026 · COMPARISON / Vendor Comparison

Finance leaders often land on this comparison after realizing their current ERP is either too big or too small. Sage Intacct and NetSuite are both cloud financial systems, but they are not the same animal. NetSuite, owned by Oracle, is a full ERP with CRM, inventory, and order management built in. Intacct, part of Sage, is a financial management platform that attaches to other systems, including Salesforce or several ERPs. That difference drives almost every decision that follows.

For a services business that lives in Salesforce and doesn't hold inventory, Intacct is usually the better fit. Its dimensions and allocations let you report on projects, locations, departments, and classes without messy account codes. You build a chart of accounts once and tag entries with as much detail as you like. NetSuite can do this too, but its native advantages only appear when you need NetSuite to handle the entire operational record — sales orders, inventory receipts, manufacturing, and then the financial close on the same database.

NetSuite's breadth cuts both ways. Because it is one system, you avoid the cost and risk of plugging together separate apps. But you pay for that in implementation complexity. Projects can run long, and the SuiteScript customization platform makes it tempting to build what your old system did instead of changing process. Intacct starts from a tighter accounting core, so the first deployment is often faster — but you have to manage integrations to non-financial systems, and those integrations can be where budgets quietly disappear.

Pricing is harder to compare than the brochures suggest. NetSuite typically sells annual subscriptions based on modules and user tiers, and the deal often comes wrapped in multi-year terms. Intacct pricing varies by edition, number of modules, and users, with add-ons like project accounting or multi-entity consolidation. Neither is cheap, and both vendors negotiate, so the only reliable number comes from a written quote.
