Dynamics 365 vs SAP Business One: Which ERP Wins?

PUBLISHED August 28, 2026 · COMPARISON / Comparison

The comparison usually starts with a false premise. SAP Business One and Microsoft Dynamics 365 are not the same kind of product, and pretending otherwise leads to bad selections. SAP B1 is a single, vertically oriented ERP for small and mid-sized companies, often on-premise. Dynamics 365 is a portfolio of modular cloud applications, built to scale across divisions and geographies. That difference shapes everything: implementation, pricing, integration, and how much your IT team will hate or love you.

SAP B1 has real strengths for manufacturing and distribution. Strong inventory functions, production planning, and localisation for dozens of countries. But the roadmap is cautious, and some clients feel SAP's focus has shifted to S/4HANA, leaving B1 as a cash cow. The partner ecosystem is deep, so you can find experts, but customisation is expensive and upgrades require testing beyond what many mid-market teams expect. In practice, B1 works well if you run a discrete manufacturer that wants a fixed process and does not plan to radically change it.

Dynamics 365 is a broader bet. The Finance and Operations app can substitute for a tier-one ERP, but the licensing is more complicated than B1's. You need to choose between Business Central and Finance and Operations, and the gap between them is wider than Microsoft's marketing admits. Business Central is the actual successor to NAV, good for small and mid-market commerce and light manufacturing. Finance and Operations is heavier, suited to complex supply chains and international consolidation. Both integrate with the Microsoft stack, which is useful if you live in Teams, Excel, and Azure. It is less useful if you are not already a Microsoft shop.

The decision should not start with feature lists. Start with your current ERP, your internal technical capacity, and your growth trajectory. B1 is safer if you want a predictable, process-centric system and a partner who can manage the deployment. Dynamics 365 offers more headroom but demands more from your team during implementation. Also consider the cost of leaving. B1 is notoriously sticky; data extraction and process re-mapping can sting. Dynamics is also not a walk away, but its modular structure at least lets you retire pieces gradually. Neither vendor publishes all-in pricing, and in both cases the total cost depends on the partner, the configuration, and the size of the deployment. Get a detailed quote with support and upgrade costs included before committing.
