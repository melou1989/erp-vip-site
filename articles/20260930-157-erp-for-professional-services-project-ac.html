ERP for Professional Services: Project Accounting Needs

PUBLISHED September 30, 2026 · INSIGHTS / Project Accounting

Professional services firms aren't manufacturers or retailers. Their product is people's time and expertise. A generic ERP built for inventory or discrete manufacturing will choke on project accounting. I've watched a consulting firm try to run multi-phase engagements through a system that only understood cost centers. Revenue recognition became a manual spreadsheet nightmare, and project managers had no idea if they were profitable until months later.

What project accounting actually requires is a project dimension that flows through time entry, expenses, procurement, billing, and revenue. Time tracking must distinguish billable, non-billable, and internal projects. Expenses need allocation, markup, or pass-through treatment. Billing can be T&M, fixed fee, milestone, retainer, or a mix. Each method changes how you recognize revenue. Under ASC 606 and IFRS 15, you're constantly estimating progress and adjusting. Generic ERP modules often handle one or two billing methods, then force manual workarounds for the rest.

Revenue recognition is where most ERPs fail. Professional services firms don't recognize revenue when they invoice. They recognize it as work is performed, often using percentage of completion or effort expended. The system must capture hours, costs, and progress, then calculate recognized and deferred revenue. It must also handle contract modifications and change orders without breaking the audit trail. I've seen firms buy standalone revenue recognition tools because their ERP couldn't do it. That's a sign of a bad fit.

Resource management and utilization matter just as much. A project accounting system should tell you who is available, what skills they have, and how booked they are. Utilization rates drive profitability. If your ERP can't show real-time utilization by person, practice, or project, you're flying blind. Some ERPs have added resource management, but it's often bolted on. PSA tools like Kantata, Certinia, or Deltek excel here. Many firms run a PSA for resource and project management, then sync to an ERP for financials. That two-system approach works, but integration costs and data latency are real.

When you evaluate ERP for professional services, don't just look at the general ledger. Ask for a demo of a fixed-fee project with a change order, a T&M project with a cap, and a retainer that rolls over. See how the system handles WIP, revenue recognition, and billing. Check how time and expenses flow into the project. If the vendor shows you a generic project costing module, walk away. Pricing varies significantly by vendor and deployment size, but the cost of a bad fit is far higher than the license fees.
