ERP for Distribution: Warehouse & Logistics Integration

PUBLISHED August 02, 2026 · INSIGHTS / Integration Strategy

Most ERP selection processes for distributors start in finance. Order entry, AP/AR, general ledger — that's where the slides live. Warehouse and logistics integration is where projects tend to slow down. It's not an accident. ERP systems are built from the ledger outward. Inventory is an asset on the income statement. In the warehouse, it's a carton on a pallet in row C, bin 12, subject to humidity, peeling labels, and forklift damage.

The original design tension never disappears. A finance system wants to move costs when a transaction is posted. A warehouse moves material based on physical priorities: wave pick scheduling, truck door assignments, rework, quarantined stock. When those don't align, you get cycle count adjustments and negative inventory. Many distribution ERP complaints are not really about the software. They're about the mental model behind the software.

Vendors have different levels of seriousness here. SAP and Oracle have deep logistics options, but you have to pay for and configure them. Infor products like CloudSuite Distribution are built for the sector from the start. Microsoft Dynamics 365 Supply Chain Management offers warehouse management features, but the boundaries between what's in the product and what needs an ISV license aren't always obvious. NetSuite's built-in WMS is adequate for simple operations but gets thin around complex putaway rules or high-volume parcel shipping. Honestly, demos rarely show these details. You have to ask for them.

Ask how a back-ordered line behaves when a partial shipment arrives and a carrier trailer is waiting. Ask how lot numbers are handled when a warehouse worker mixes two purchase orders in a single bin. Multiple locations, intercompany transfers, freight claims — these are the processes that separate an ERP that supports distribution from one that merely tolerates it. If the demo can't answer that in a straight line, that's the answer.
