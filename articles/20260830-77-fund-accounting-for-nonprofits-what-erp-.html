Fund Accounting for Nonprofits: What ERP Buyers Need to Know

PUBLISHED August 30, 2026 · INSIGHTS / Buyer's Guide

For-profit ERP software is built around one accounting equation: revenue minus expenses equals profit. Nonprofits don't have owners expecting a return. They have donors, foundations, and government agencies that attach strings to their money. That small difference cascades through every module — chart of accounts, budgeting, purchasing, reporting. In practice, a nonprofit that uses a commercial ERP often ends up contorting its chart of accounts into a secret code, embedding the fund in the department or project segment, then fighting the general ledger the rest of the year.

Fund accounting treats the accounting system as a collection of self-balancing funds. Each fund has its own assets, liabilities, net assets, revenue, and expenses. A restricted grant for a food pantry is one fund; an unrestricted donation for operations is another. The goal is to prove to auditors and funders that you spent the money exactly as required. If you buy one laptop with unrestricted funds and another with a restricted grant, the ERP needs to keep those two purchases from ever being casually merged.

What to look for in an ERP? First, native support for the nonprofit chart of accounts — where the fund balance carries forward year to year, and where expenses are tracked by natural class, project, and fund as distinct segments. Second, allocation capabilities: shared costs like rent, insurance, and IT need to be distributed across funding sources according to an allocation method that you can defend. Third, the ability to produce FASB-compliant financial statements: net assets with donor restrictions, without donor restrictions, and the statement of activities. Some general-purpose ERPs can do this with heavy customization, but that customization becomes your permanent weather condition.

Vendors vary. Blackbaud Financial Edge NXT is built for fund accounting, but it's part of a donor-focused ecosystem that may feel narrow if your operations are complex. Sage Intacct has strong fund accounting capabilities through its nonprofit edition, including multi-fund reporting and grant management. Oracle NetSuite and Microsoft Dynamics 365 are more generalists; they can serve bigger nonprofits but they're not designed around the fund balance. The right choice depends on how much of your budget is restricted, how many grant entities you have, and how much your finance team wants to fight the software.

Pricing varies significantly by vendor, deployment size, and module configuration. The more important cost is the one you pay after go-live: the monthly close. If the system can't handle fund balances naturally, your senior accountant will be building spreadsheets to reconstruct what happened. At least in the nonprofit world, the whole point of an ERP is to retain the story of where each dollar came from and what it was allowed to do.
