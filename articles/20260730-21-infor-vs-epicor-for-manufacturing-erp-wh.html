Infor vs Epicor for Manufacturing ERP: Which Mid-Market Suite Wins?

PUBLISHED July 30, 2026 · COMPARISON / Vendor Comparison

Pick the wrong manufacturing ERP and you’ll spend years fighting your own software. Infor and Epicor are two of the most common contenders for mid-market discrete and process manufacturers, but they approach the same problems from opposite directions. Infor pushes hard on industry-specific data models and a single-tenant-or-cloud strategy tied to AWS. Epicor, especially with its Kinetic platform, bets on configurability and a more traditional partner ecosystem. Neither is a safe default—each demands a clear understanding of how your shop floor actually works.

Infor's CloudSuite Industrial (the old MAPICS / Syteline lineage) leans into process manufacturing: batch tracking, formula management, lot control. If you make chemicals, food, or pharmaceuticals, the out‑of‑the‑box data structures save months of customization. The trade‑off is a steeper learning curve and a less forgiving upgrade path—Infor’s cloud version locks you into their multi‑tenant instance on AWS, which can limit integrations with legacy plant‑floor systems. Epicor’s Kinetic (formerly Epicor ERP) shines in make‑to‑order, job shops, and metal fabrication. Its estimation engine and project‑centric costing are genuinely useful for anyone quoting custom work. Epicor also gives you more deployment flexibility—cloud, hosted, or on‑prem—without forcing a rewrite of custom reports.

Both vendors have been through ownership changes. Infor is part of Koch Industries, which gives it deep pockets but also a corporate drive to standardize around the Infor OS (their middleware layer). That OS can feel like extra overhead if you just want a solid MRP. Epicor, now backed by CD&R, has been consolidating its product lines—absorbing the old Epicor 9, Vantage, and iScala into Kinetic. That consolidation has been messy; some long‑time users still grumble about the pace of feature parity. Pricing for both is hard to pin down publicly, but expect Infor’s annual subscription to be on the higher side for the full CloudSuite, while Epicor tends to be more negotiable, especially if you keep some modules on‑prem.

Implementation timelines vary more by partner than by vendor. Infor’s channel is thinner and more selective—you often end up working directly with Infor Consulting Services, which can be rigorous but expensive. Epicor’s partner network is broader, which means you can find a shop that knows your vertical, but quality ranges wildly. The real deciding factor should be your production type: if you need deep formula‑based manufacturing or highly regulated traceability, start with Infor. If you’re a job shop, a fabricator, or a make‑to‑order operation where every part number is a custom configuration, Epicor will frustrate you less. Both can be made to work; the question is how much of your budget you want to burn on consultants forcing a square peg into a round hole.
