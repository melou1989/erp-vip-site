ERP and Sustainability Reporting: What's Changing

PUBLISHED September 24, 2026 · INSIGHTS / Compliance

Sustainability reporting used to live in a spreadsheet owned by one unlucky person in sustainability. That era is ending. CSRD, ISSB, and California's disclosure laws push companies toward auditable, repeatable ESG data. The data has to come from somewhere: procurement, production, logistics, HR, finance. Which means ERP.

The change isn't that ERP vendors added an ESG tab. It's that carbon and energy data are being tied to transactions. A purchase order for steel has a supplier-specific emissions factor. A shipment has a mode and distance. An electricity invoice has meter and location data. Finance wants that mapped to the general ledger for audit. Operations wants it without duplicating every entry. That's harder than it sounds.

SAP is embedding carbon accounting into S/4HANA with Green Ledger and Sustainability Control Tower. Oracle Fusion Cloud Sustainability, Microsoft Sustainability Manager, and Workday's ESG reporting cover overlapping ground. Infor, IFS, and others have ESG functionality or partner apps, often through consolidation, procurement, or HCM. The line between ERP, EPM, and dedicated ESG tools is blurry.

The hard part is not the dashboard. It's the data lineage. Assurers will ask how a number was calculated, which emission factor was used, who changed it, and what period it covers. Spreadsheets can't answer that cheaply. ERP can, if configured well. But most ERPs weren't designed for scope 3. Supplier data arrives late, in different formats, with gaps. So vendors rely on estimates and secondary factors. That's acceptable for some disclosures, not for others.

Ask vendors: Can you capture activity data at the transaction level? How do you handle unit conversions, restatements, and factor versioning? Can we consolidate across entities and currencies? Does the audit trail meet assurance standards? How do you ingest utility, travel, and supplier data? What's native versus partner-built? The answers vary a lot. Pricing varies significantly by vendor and deployment size, and scope 3 often pushes projects outside the original budget.

ERP-led ESG reporting changes roles. Controllers get emissions in the close. Procurement gets supplier engagement targets. IT gets another integration surface. Sustainability teams stop being report writers and start being data stewards. That shift is messy. The vendors are moving. The data model is the part buyers still control.
