Dynamics GP vs Business Central: Migration Reality

PUBLISHED September 07, 2026 · COMPARISON / Comparison Guide

If you're still evaluating Dynamics GP as a future platform, that decision has effectively been made for you. Microsoft has announced a wind-down that will stop new GP license sales altogether, and while existing customers can still run the product and get support, the roadmap is visible from orbit: everything leads to Dynamics 365 Business Central. The real question isn't whether to move — it's whether you start planning now or get forced against a deadline later.

The uncomfortable technical truth is that GP and Business Central are not two versions of the same system. GP is an on-premises product with decades of embedded design around SQL Server databases, Dexterity code, and a wide, quirky ecosystem of third-party add-ons. Business Central is a cloud-native SaaS application with an extension architecture using a completely different programming language, AL, and a managed Microsoft environment. That means there is no upgrade path. There is only a data migration combined with a process re-implementation. Calling the move an upgrade misleads everyone in the room.

What actually breaks in practice? Start with reporting. GP shops often rely on customized SQL reports, Dexterity-written inquiries, and integrations that reach directly into the database. Business Central doesn't let you touch its tables that way. It exposes APIs, but every query and report that bypassed the application layer is now unsupported. Similarly, many specialized GP ISVs still don't have a Business Central version of their product. Your warehouse or industry-specific module might simply not exist in the new stack, meaning you'll have to evaluate replacements while your converted data is still settling in.

Business Central does deliver things GP never will. Subscription pricing spreads cost and shifts the burden of upgrades and infrastructure back to Microsoft. The Power Platform integration is substantial — you can tie Dynamics 365 sales, finance roles, and Power BI in ways GP can't compete with. Bank feeds, approval workflows, and multi-company consolidation are native, and the mobile experience isn't a third-party bolt-on. But those benefits assume you can clean up the messy, decades-old chart of accounts, segment logic, and historical transactions living in every GP installation. That cleanup is the core project, whether you want it to be or not.

CFOs and IT directors should start by cataloging every Dexterity customization, SQL Server job, and piece of code integrated with GP. Then ask which of those actually map to a current business process — not a process you remember from 2010. Many GP installations carry customizations nobody uses anymore, and those are the easiest to kill. The ones that matter will need a functional equivalent in Business Central, either from Microsoft, an ISV, or a partner extension. Run a pilot with real data, not sample data. If you wait until Microsoft gives you a hard deadline, you inherit the risk of a rushed migration. If you begin now, you get to set the timeline.
