ERP Security Best Practices for Finance Teams

PUBLISHED August 22, 2026 · GUIDE / Checklist

Most ERP systems ship with security settings that make life easy for the IT deployment team and easy for attackers, too. Default admin credentials, permissive roles, and open integration interfaces are common in fresh installations. Finance teams should never assume that the ERP vendor's default configuration meets their security requirements. It usually doesn't.

The biggest threat to financial data in an ERP is often far less exotic than an external hack. It is the user with conflicting responsibilities — someone who can create a supplier and also approve their invoice, or enter a journal entry and post it without review. That is a segregation-of-duties conflict, and auditors will flag it before an attacker ever goes near the system. An effective practice is to run a segregation-of-duties analysis on user roles quarterly, either using the ERP's built-in controls or purpose-built tools, and to correct conflicts before they become audit findings.

Access reviews are another area where finance teams need to be hands-on, not just leave it to IT. Every user with access to the finance module should have their rights recertified by the finance controller or CFO at least quarterly. That includes checking whether former employees' accounts are still active — and deactivating them immediately, not waiting for the next scheduled review. Credentials from a departed accountant are one of the most common ways attackers slip into ERP systems, especially when the user had access to payroll or payment files. Enable multi-factor authentication for every login, with no exceptions for vendors who connect to the system for EDI or invoice submission.

Finally, review the audit logs before you need them. Automated alerts for pattern anomalies — journal entries posted outside working hours, repeated failed logins, or changes to vendor bank accounts — are worth configuring. And remember that ERPs are on-prem or cloud, and the security responsibilities differ wildly. Cloud vendors handle the infrastructure, but you still control user access, segregation of duties, and how integrations are configured. Patch management is also your job regardless: a finance system that is months behind on patches is a finance system that is one vendor announcement away from being compromised.
